How the in-ear EEG classifier works

The classifier comes out of my master's thesis on detecting anxiety and depression from EEG, and the paper that grew from it. It takes a resting-state recording from a single in-ear electrode and says whether it looks more like the healthy or the pathological group in the training data. This page covers what that means, how well it works and where it doesn't.

Using it

  1. Record resting-state EEG with an IDUN Guardian in-ear device, ideally a few minutes with eyes closed or open.
  2. Export the recording as CSV. The app reads the ch1 column, sampled at 250 Hz.
  3. Drop the file onto the app. The recording is split into 10-second segments, each segment is classified, and the majority vote is the output.

The research

Master's thesis, Faculty of Information Technology, Brno University of Technology (2026), and the manuscript Detecting Anxiety and Depression from In-Ear and Eight-Channel EEG Using Deep Learning: A Multi-Dataset Study, which is not yet published.

The study combines three resting-state datasets into one corpus of 169 subjects: people with depression, people with anxiety, people with both, and healthy controls. It then compares eight-channel models with models that see a single in-ear channel. Its main finding is that one in-ear channel loses little against eight scalp electrodes. The code is on GitHub.

The model

The app runs CNN-AttnS, the best in-ear model in the paper. It has 79 K parameters. Two convolutional layers turn a segment's spectrogram into ten time steps, and a single self-attention layer looks across them before a small classifier decides between healthy and pathological (depression, anxiety or both).

The paper evaluates it with 10-fold, subject-independent cross-validation: ten models, each tested on subjects it never saw in training. The paper reports their average. A web page needs one model, so this one runs fold 5:

Fold 5 was picked over the others because:

  • It trained properly. Two folds reached their best validation score in the first few epochs (2 and 6), which says more about luck than learning.
  • It keeps the paper's trade-off: somewhat more sensitive than specific. The fold closest to the average overall, fold 3, reverses it.
  • Every metric is within 0.7 standard deviations of the paper's average.

Fold 5's numbers come from its 727 held-out segments and sit slightly above average, so the paper's average is the better guide to what to expect.

What happens to your recording

  1. The whole signal is z-scored and linearly detrended.
  2. A 4th-order Butterworth band-pass keeps 1–70 Hz, and a notch filter removes 50 Hz mains hum.
  3. It is cut into non-overlapping 10-second segments. A shorter tail is dropped. Each segment is z-scored again.
  4. Each segment becomes a log-magnitude spectrogram: 256-sample Hamming windows with 192 samples of overlap, 0–70 Hz, 72 × 41 values. This is what the app draws.
  5. The network classifies each segment, and the class with the most segments is the output.

Every step is a JavaScript port of the thesis code in Python. Automated tests compare each stage, and the network's outputs, against the Python version on the same input. The network runs with ONNX Runtime Web on WebAssembly.

Limits

This is a research prototype, not a medical device. Its output is not a diagnosis and must not be used to make decisions about anyone's health.

  • It was trained on 169 subjects from research datasets and has never been validated clinically.
  • Most training data was a surrogate in-ear channel computed from scalp electrodes (T8 − T7). On genuine IDUN in-ear recordings, the kind the app accepts, cross-validated segment accuracy was about 67%, and only about half of the healthy segments were recognised as healthy.
  • "Pathological" means resembling the depression and anxiety groups in these datasets. It does not distinguish between the two.
  • Training discarded segments with a low IDUN signal-quality score. The app has no quality file, so noisy segments are classified anyway.
  • Other devices, other sampling rates or recordings with heavy movement give meaningless output.

Privacy

Your recording never leaves your device: the file is read, filtered and classified inside the browser tab, and nothing is uploaded or stored. The site has no analytics. The only outside request is for the typeface, from Google Fonts.
